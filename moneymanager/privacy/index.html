The short version

Amais: Money Manager is a personal finance tracker built around a simple idea: your financial records live on your device, and anything that leaves it is either something you explicitly asked for or the minimum needed to run the optional services described below.

  • Your accounts and transactions are stored locally on your device. We do not operate a server that stores your financial records in plain, readable form. There is no manual export/backup file feature in this version of the App.
  • Signing in is optional. You can use every core feature of the App — creating accounts, logging transactions, viewing your history — without ever signing in. If you choose to sign in with Google or Apple, we (via Firebase Authentication) receive your name, email address, profile photo, and a unique identifier for your account. Signing in does not copy your local financial data anywhere.
  • A $5/year premium subscription removes ads and unlocks encrypted cloud backup. Purchases are handled by Apple, and we use RevenueCat to track your subscription status — we never see your card details.
  • Premium cloud sync is end-to-end encrypted with a password only you know. If you turn it on, your accounts and transactions are backed up to Google's Firestore database — but only as scrambled ciphertext. We, Google, and anyone else who might access our systems cannot read it. If you forget your encryption password and lose access to the device that has it, that backup cannot be recovered by anyone, including us. See Section 8.
  • Free users may see a rewarded video ad (via Google's AdMob) at two points in the App — saving a new account and saving a new transaction — currently on iOS only. On iOS, we ask for your permission (Apple's App Tracking Transparency prompt) before any advertising identifier is used. See Section 9 for what this means, including a region we haven't fully built consent tooling for yet.
  • Signing out gives you a choice: keep your local data on the device, or delete it. Read that choice carefully before you tap anything — see Section 6.3, because deleting is immediate and cannot be undone by us.
  • We do not sell your data, and we do not run analytics or crash-reporting software of our own in the App.

This summary is provided for convenience only. It is not a substitute for the full policy below, which governs.

6. Local data, and the optional account you can sign in with

6.1 Your financial records — always local

Every account and transaction you create — title, balance, amounts payable/receivable, investment values, transaction details, tags, dates — is stored in a SQLite database inside the App's private, sandboxed storage on your device. This is true whether or not you sign in, and whether or not you subscribe. Signing in does not upload this data anywhere by itself — only the separate, opt-in cloud sync feature in Section 8 does that, and only if you turn it on.

Free-text fields. The description you type on a transaction, and the title you give an account, are free text. Be mindful of what you type there — see Section 8.1 for how that text is handled if you enable cloud sync.

You can sign in with your Google account, or (on iOS) with Sign in with Apple. Signing in is entirely optional and does not gate any part of the App — every screen works fully whether you're signed in or not.

6.3 Signing out — read this before you tap "Delete Data"

Because the App's local database is not automatically split per signed-in user (it's one shared store on the device), when you sign out we ask you a real question, not a formality:

"Do you want to delete the accounts and transactions stored on this device, or keep them for the next person who signs in?"

  • Keep Data — your local accounts and transactions stay on the device exactly as they are, visible to whoever uses the App on it next (signed in or not).
  • Delete Data — every account and transaction currently stored on this device is permanently erased, immediately, before you're signed out. This cannot be undone by us — we hold no copy.

This choice only affects the local copy on this device. If you had premium cloud sync (Section 8) turned on, your encrypted backup in the cloud is not touched by this local deletion either way — it stays exactly as it was until you separately manage or delete it.

8. Premium cloud sync — end-to-end encrypted

If you're a signed-in, premium subscriber, you can turn on an optional backup of your accounts and transactions to Google's Cloud Firestore database (part of the same Firebase platform used for sign-in). This is off by default and only reachable behind the premium paywall.

8.1 How the encryption works, and why we can't read your data

The first time you turn sync on, the App asks you to create a separate encryption password — not your sign-in password, and something we never see or store:

  • Your password is run through a key-derivation function (Argon2id) on your device to produce an encryption key. The password itself is never transmitted anywhere.
  • Every account and transaction is encrypted on your device (AES-256-GCM) before it is sent to Firestore. What actually reaches Google's servers is unreadable ciphertext.
  • Alongside the ciphertext, Firestore also stores a small amount of non-secret metadata needed to re-derive the same key on another device (a random salt and the key-derivation parameters — not the password), and a plaintext last-modified timestamp for each record, used only to decide which copy is newer if the same record was edited on two devices.
  • Nobody but you holds the password — not us, not Google/Firebase, not RevenueCat. We have no technical means to decrypt your data, recover a forgotten password, or read your financial records from the server.

If you forget this password and lose access to every device that has it saved, your encrypted cloud backup cannot be recovered — by us, by Google, or by anyone. This is a direct consequence of how the encryption is designed: there is no "reset password" option that works, because resetting it would require someone other than you to be able to derive your key, which would defeat the point of end-to-end encryption. If you find yourself in this situation, the only option the App offers is to permanently delete the old encrypted backup and start a fresh one — see the next paragraph.

If you can't remember your password, the App offers an "I don't have this password" option, which permanently deletes your entire existing encrypted backup from our systems and lets you set up a new password with a clean slate. This does not touch your local data on the device you're using at the time.

8.2 What we (and Google, as our processor) can see

Firestore, and anyone with server-side access to it, can see: your Firebase UID (which record belongs to which account), the ciphertext blobs themselves (unreadable without your password), the non-secret encryption metadata described above, and the plaintext timestamp of when each record was last changed. At no point does plaintext of your account names, balances, transaction amounts, or transaction descriptions reach our servers or Google's.

Access to your own namespace in Firestore is restricted to your signed-in Firebase account only (enforced server-side).

8.3 Turning sync off

Turning the sync toggle off in Settings stops any further pushing/pulling — it does not delete your existing encrypted backup. To permanently delete it, use the "I don't have this password" flow described above, or contact us at sheikhamais@gmail.com and we will delete your entire Firestore namespace (the ciphertext, since we cannot read it, but we can still delete it) on request.

16. Security

We take appropriate technical and organisational measures required by Article 32 GDPR and comparable laws:

  • Local data sits in the App's private, sandboxed storage, protected by the OS and by device-level file encryption when your device is locked with a passcode.
  • The optional cloud sync feature (Section 8) is designed so that plaintext financial data never reaches our servers or Google's — only ciphertext, non-secret key-derivation metadata, and last-modified timestamps do. Your locally cached sync key is stored using the OS's secure credential store (iOS Keychain / Android Keystore via flutter_secure_storage), not in plain app storage.
  • Payment processing is handled entirely by Apple; we hold no payment data. RevenueCat handles entitlement data under its own security programme.
  • Access to our email account is protected by a strong, unique password and multi-factor authentication. The web pages hosting this policy are served over HTTPS.

No method of transmission or storage is completely secure, and we cannot guarantee absolute security. We encourage you to protect your device with a passcode or biometric lock, keep your OS up to date, and choose a strong, memorable encryption password if you enable cloud sync — see the irreversibility warning in Section 8.1.

Breach notification. In the unlikely event of a personal data breach likely to result in a risk to your rights and freedoms, we will notify the competent supervisory authority within 72 hours of becoming aware of it (Article 33 GDPR) and affected individuals without undue delay where required.
